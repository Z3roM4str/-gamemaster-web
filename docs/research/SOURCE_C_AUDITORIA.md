# Source C — Auditoría del informe anterior

Source-derived companion to the owner-supplied 13-page PDF `1. Auditoría del informe anterior.pdf`.

This source is especially important because it audits earlier operational statements and separates what it considers demonstrated, supported, inferential, speculative or unresolved.

## 1. Purpose of the audit

The report revisits earlier claims about chromostereopsis and asks whether they are actually supported by the cited literature.

It should be used to **reduce overclaiming**, not to erase the earlier research history.

## 2. Claims the audit treats as comparatively strong

### Large spectral separation

The audit treats large wavelength separation, especially red/blue, as a strong condition for producing chromostereoscopic depth.

### Red/blue as the conventional strong pair

The audit presents saturated red and blue as the main high-contrast pair used in the cited literature.

### Dark background as a strong practical condition

This audit version is more affirmative than Source A about dark/black backgrounds. It describes dark backgrounds as strongly favorable in the cited conditions.

**Important repository rule:** Source A later warns against turning “black is best” into a universal law. GameMaster therefore uses near-black by design while preserving the scientific uncertainty.

### Saturated colors

The audit treats vivid/saturated colors as favorable and common in strong demonstrations.

### Crisp edges

Defined edges and clearly segmented chromatic regions are treated as supported or strongly plausible for isolating the effect.

### Typical red-forward / blue-rear direction

The audit treats this as the majority condition in the cited setup, while acknowledging individual variation elsewhere in the research set.

## 3. Major correction: equiluminance

The audit specifically challenges the earlier idea that equiluminance is automatically optimal for maximum perceived depth.

Its interpretation is that equalizing luminance can reduce the apparent effect in some cited demonstrations, whereas luminance differences can strengthen the overall depth impression.

At the same time, equal luminance remains useful scientifically when the purpose is to isolate the chromatic component from brightness cues.

**GameMaster implementation rule:**

- do not claim equal luminance is the strongest artistic configuration;
- do not claim unequal luminance is universally optimal either;
- tune red/blue brightness intentionally for the actual interface and comfort.

## 4. Claims the audit downgrades or leaves unresolved

The report explicitly identifies several earlier recommendations as unsupported, inferential or still experimental:

- anti-aliasing as a quantified problem;
- repetition as an automatic enhancer;
- one ideal medium spatial frequency;
- one optimal element density;
- black outlines as specifically beneficial;
- Op Art as intrinsically superior;
- technical/vector drawing as perceptually superior;
- AI-friendliness as a perceptual argument;
- universal line thickness values;
- universal density rules.

These may still be useful design hypotheses, but Codex must not present them as established science.

## 5. Perceptual Constraint Set v2

The audit organizes constraints into strong, contextual, unknown and aesthetic-freedom groups.

### Strong

- large red/blue spectral separation;
- strong chromatic contrast;
- dark background as a practical strong condition in the cited setups;
- defined boundaries / clear segmentation.

### Contextual

- luminance;
- observer adaptation and pupil behavior;
- display/print medium;
- viewing distance / scale;
- binocular visual condition.

### Unknown / experimental

- exact spatial frequency optimum;
- density of elements;
- repetition/periodicity;
- additional contours;
- superposition/transparency effects in complex artistic scenes.

### Aesthetic freedoms

The audit explicitly allows broad formal exploration as long as the core chromatic separation is not destroyed:

- geometric or organic forms;
- abstract or figurative representation;
- light textures/tramas that preserve color separation;
- variable module sizes;
- layered composition without accidentally replacing the chromatic effect with ordinary perspective/shadow.

### Geometry

### Graphic unit

- points;
- lines;
- bands;
- polygons;
- cells;

- modules;

### Structure

- grids;
- tessellations;
- trees/branching;
- vector fields;

### Representation

- abstract;
- ornamental;
- cartographic;
- scientific;

### Density and regularity

The audit encourages exploring sparse to dense and deterministic to stochastic compositions rather than assuming one density/repetition level is optimal.

### Chromatic treatment

It considers:

- colored line systems;

## 7. Territories the audit identifies as promising

Examples include:

- non-periodic tessellations such as Penrose-like systems;
- hierarchical Voronoi;
- L-system / dendritic structures;
- vector flow fields;
- abstract topographic maps;
- parametric architectural patterns;
- modular ornament;
- circuit/network diagrams;
- natural fractal structures;
- coded typography/symbol systems.

These are **candidate territories**, not experimentally proven winners.

## 8. White-space areas highlighted by the audit

The source argues that more distinctive opportunities may exist in:

- nonperiodic tessellations;
- organic generative fractals;
- abstract scientific diagrams;
- coded alphabets / symbolic systems;
- vector flow art;
- parametric architecture;
- abstract cultural ornament;
- cellular automata / reaction-diffusion;
- imaginary maps;
- typography-science hybrids.

This is relevant to GameMaster because a strong brand identity should remain recognizable even without depending only on the red/blue palette.

## 9. Eight candidate systems proposed for visualization

The audit lists example systems such as:

1. non-periodic red/blue tessellation;
2. L-system tree/fractal;
3. topographic map coding;
4. vector circuit design;
5. modular arabesque/ornament;
6. generative flow field;
7. biomorphic Voronoi;
8. modular abstract typography.

The report discusses different strengths for perceptual potential, independent identity, AI control, procedural control and print reproduction.

Those comparisons are design judgments rather than direct scientific experiments.

## 10. Explicit warning about anaglyph resemblance

The audit identifies a risk that certain red/blue treatments may resemble conventional anaglyph graphics, especially when designs imitate duplicated contours or familiar magenta/cyan-style separation.

This is directly relevant to the current GameMaster correction.

**Mandatory interpretation:** do not implement “depth” by producing displaced copies of one contour. Use distinct chromatic regions and independent structures.

## 11. Hybrid directions proposed

Examples include:

- fractal + circuit;
- tessellation + ornament;
- map + L-system.

The value is not merely decorative novelty. The source is looking for a repeatable system that combines macrostructure and microstructure while preserving clean chromatic planes.

## 12. Decisive recommendations from the audit

### What it treats as known enough to use

- large red/blue chromatic separation;
- vivid colors;
- dark backgrounds as a strong starting context;
- crisp, discrete regions.

### What it says is excessive to claim

- equiluminance is always optimal;
- one universal line thickness;
- one universal density;
- geometric forms are always superior to organic ones;
- a particular named style is scientifically best.

### What it says still needs testing

- spatial frequency;
- density;
- complex superpositions/transparencies;
- exact monitor/print behavior;
- individual differences;
- candidate-vs-candidate rankings.

## 13. GameMaster implications from Source C

### Use as high-confidence design direction

- clean red/blue separation;
- dark/near-black brand canvas;
- hard boundaries in chromostereoscopic focal modules;
- conventional semantic layout underneath the visual effect.

### Use as exploratory identity territory

- topography;
- circuit diagrams;
- non-periodic tessellation;
- modular symbol systems;
- generative flow fields;
- controlled ornament;
- fractal/natural structures.

### Do not infer

- that a red/blue offset shadow is chromostereopsis;
- that black, maximum saturation or one luminance relation is universally optimal;
- that more repetition/density automatically means stronger depth;
- that one visual language has been scientifically validated as the best.
